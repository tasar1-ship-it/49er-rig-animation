In words

Measure your own centre of mass: the plank test
  1. Rest one end of a stiff plank on a broomstick and the other end on a bathroom scale. Measure the distance between the two supports.
  2. Read the scale with the plank empty.
  3. Lie on the plank, straight, soles exactly above the broomstick, arms where they are when you trapeze. Read the scale again.
  4. Second reading minus first, times the distance between supports, divided by your weight. That is how far your centre of mass is from your soles.
  5. Divide by your height and put that number in the slider on the left.
What this is, and is not

Righting moment here is weight times the horizontal distance from the centreline to each sailor's centre of mass, with the boat flat. The trapeze wire does not appear: wire tension and foot pressure are internal to boat plus crew, so hook height and wire angle change the load on the legs and the mast, not the righting moment.

Lever is feet distance + (share × height + arm shift) × cos(angle). An arm stretched overhead moves about 5 percent of body mass along the body; the shift used is 2.3 percent of height for each arm. Both figures come from standard body-segment tables and are estimates for any one person.

Left out: heel, bent knees, a curved back, kit carried away from the torso, and anything dynamic. The class beam of 2.9 m is the builders' figure. Use the numbers to compare, not as a measurement of the boat.
