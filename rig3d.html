The same manual says not to chase main shroud tension: get the forestay right, then set uppers and lowers so the main sits at the right depth. It also says this rig is sensitive to lowers tension.

What this model is, and is not

A beam with two bending planes, pinned at the foot, held by seven tension-only wires that slide over rigid spreader tips. Turning an adjuster changes a wire's length; tensions, rake and bend follow from equilibrium. Every wire can go slack.

The layout follows the Southern Spars manual: three mast sections, two identical swept spreader sets at the joints, forestay just above the top spreader, shrouds just below it, uppers bolted into the top section, lowers into the bottom section.

Estimated, not measured: all heights, chainplate positions, spreader sweep, tube and wire stiffness, and the size and split of the sailing loads. Left out: mast compression adding to bend, spreader flex, hull flex, the cunningham. So the directions and the couplings are physical, the millimetres are model values, and none of it is a setting to copy onto the boat.
